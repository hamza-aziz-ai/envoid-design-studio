What you are seeing

The sun sits exactly where it would over Sector 5, Vaishali at the date and time you choose, calculated in your browser with the same astronomical equations as the sunlight explorer. Light comes in through the window and nowhere else, so the patch on the floor, the shadow of the window bars and the way the sofa catches the light are all the real geometry of that moment.

Try a south-facing window at noon on the shortest day, then the longest. In December the sun reaches deep across the rug; in June it barely clears the sill. Turn the window to face west and play a summer afternoon to see why west glass is the hardest to live with. After sunset the pendant lamp comes on, and the same finishes read completely differently.

What it leaves out

The room stands alone, with nothing around it: no neighbouring buildings, trees, balconies or chajja to shade the window. On a real plot those often decide more than orientation does, and they are what a proper daylight study accounts for. The finishes are photographs of real materials, but a screen still cannot show sheen, warmth and texture the way a physical sample in your own light can.

Wood, marble, terrazzo, plaster and fabric textures are CC0 photographs from Poly Haven. The 3D is drawn with three.js, under the MIT licence.

Designing a home?

Where the windows go, how big they are and what shades them are decided on the plan, long before any finish is chosen. The studio works them out against the sun on your actual plot.
