Map approval in Ghaziabad

Plot and zone check, compliant drawings, the submission set, filing and follow-up — until the plan is sanctioned.

What map approval is

Before construction starts on a plot in Ghaziabad, the building plan has to be sanctioned by the Ghaziabad Development Authority. Locally it is called getting the naksha passed. The sanction is the authority's confirmation that what you intend to build complies with the building bye-laws and with the land use and density the master plan sets for that zone.

Building without it, or building something materially different from what was sanctioned, is not a paperwork problem. It exposes the structure to action by the authority and makes the building difficult to sell, mortgage or get a completion certificate for.

How the process runs

GDA operates an online building plan approval system, and a submitted drawing is checked against the applicable rules automatically before it reaches a human reviewer. That has a useful consequence: a drawing that breaches a setback, a coverage limit or a height rule does not slip through on goodwill. It is rejected, and the clock restarts.

So the work that decides the outcome happens before submission, not after.

  1. Plot and zone check. Plot size, land use, and the ground coverage, floor area ratio, setbacks, height and parking the bye-laws allow there.
  2. Design to the envelope. The plan is drawn inside what is permitted, with your brief fitted to it, rather than the reverse.
  3. Submission set. Drawings prepared to the authority's required format, with the supporting documents it asks for — ownership papers, the plot's title documents, identity documents and the applicable fees.
  4. Filing and follow-up. The application is lodged, queries are answered, and revisions are made until the plan is sanctioned.
  5. Sanction letter. Issued with conditions, a permit number and a validity date.

After the sanction — the part people miss

  • Put up the site board. Sanction conditions require a board at the site showing the approving authority, permit number, approval date, validity date and the architect's name.
  • Build what was sanctioned. Deviation is the most common reason a building later fails a completion check.
  • Watch the validity. A sanction is valid for a stated period; a plan passed and then left may have to go back.
  • Get the completion certificate. The bye-laws expect it to be obtained before the building is occupied.
  • The sanction is not a title. A sanctioned plan is a construction permission. It does not establish ownership of the land, and no one should treat it as proof of one.

What we do

We handle the whole route: the plot study, the compliant design, the submission set drawn to norms, the filing, the follow-up and the revisions, through to the sanction letter. You get the architectural design and the approval from the same studio, which removes the usual loop of a designer and a liaison agent blaming each other for a rejection.

What we will not do is promise an approval the rules do not support. If the plot cannot carry what you want, you hear it at the plot study — the cheapest possible moment.

Rules change; we check the current ones

Bye-laws, fee schedules, document checklists and the online system itself are all revised from time to time, and the development authority is the only authoritative source on them. Nothing on this page is a substitute for the authority's current requirements for your plot, and we verify those at the start of every project rather than working from last year's position.

FAQ

Questions we are asked

How long does GDA map approval take?

It depends on the completeness of the submission, the plot, and the authority's current workload, so an honest answer is a range given after we have seen your documents rather than a number on a web page. What we can say is which part is under our control: a complete, compliant, correctly formatted submission is the single biggest factor in avoiding avoidable rounds of rejection.

What documents are needed?

Broadly: proof of ownership and the plot's title documents, identity documents, the plot's measurements and location details, the drawings in the required format, and the applicable fees. The exact checklist is set by the authority and does change, so we confirm the current one for your case before filing.

What does map approval cost?

Two separate things get called cost here. There are statutory fees and charges payable to the authority, which are set by it and paid at cost by the owner, and there is our professional fee for the drawings and the process. We quote the second after seeing the plot and the scope; the first is whatever the authority's current schedule says.

Can you get a plan passed that breaks the rules?

No, and nobody reputable should offer to. The plan check is substantially automated, which makes the question moot in practice as well as improper in principle. What we can do is find the best design the rules actually permit on your plot, which is usually more than owners expect.

We have already started building without a sanction. What now?

Talk to us early rather than late, and be prepared for the possibility that part of what is built does not comply. We will tell you what the position is as we read it; where it is a legal question rather than a design one, you need a lawyer as well, and we will say so.

Is the sanction proof that we own the plot?

No. A sanction letter is a permission to construct. It is explicitly not a certificate of land ownership, and it should never be relied on as one in a purchase.

Do you do approvals outside Ghaziabad?

Yes. Noida plots are sanctioned by the Noida authority and Greater Noida plots by the Greater Noida authority, each under its own regulations. The work has the same shape; the rules differ, and we confirm the right ones for your plot.
