Design, build & turnkey

Design and construction under one contract: one point of responsibility, fewer gaps between intent and site.

One contract, one party answerable

The usual arrangement puts a gap between the people who designed a building and the people who built it. When something does not match, the gap is where the argument happens, and the client is the one standing in it. Design and build closes that gap by putting both under one contract: if the built result does not match the drawing, it is the same studio's problem.

What turnkey actually includes

  • Design and documentation, to the same standard as a design-only engagement.
  • Vendor selection. Contractors and specialists chosen and priced against a BOQ, not appointed by habit.
  • Procurement. Materials ordered against the specification, with samples approved by you before anything arrives in bulk.
  • Site supervision. Regular visits with written site reports, so progress is a document rather than a phone call.
  • Schedule and budget tracking, reported against the plan, with problems escalated before they are cast in concrete.
  • Snag list and handover. A written list, closed out before you take the keys — not after you have moved in and started finding things.

What it does not include

Worth saying plainly. Statutory fees, authority charges and government levies are paid by the owner at cost. Changes you ask for after a stage is approved are priced as variations, in writing, before they are done — the alternative is a final bill nobody can account for. And a turnkey contract does not make the schedule immune to a material you decide to change in week six.

Who this suits

Clients who do not want to become project managers: people building from another city, working owners without time for a site, families who would rather approve decisions than chase them. If you enjoy running a site and have the time for it, design-only plus your own contractor is a perfectly good and usually cheaper route, and we will say so.

Project management on its own

If the construction is already contracted elsewhere, we can take project management alone: schedules, budgets, approvals and quality checks tracked and reported, with escalation before problems set. You keep your contractor; you get someone whose job is to notice.

FAQ

Questions we are asked

How is design and build priced against hiring separately?

It is not automatically cheaper, and anyone promising that is guessing. What it reliably removes is the cost of the gap: rework when the drawing and the site disagree, delays while two parties argue, and variations nobody owns. We price the design and the execution transparently so you can see both.

Can we approve vendors and materials ourselves?

Yes. Samples are approved by you before bulk procurement, and vendor quotations are shown against the BOQ. Turnkey means we carry the coordination, not that decisions happen without you.

How do you report progress?

Written site reports on an agreed schedule, with photographs, progress against the programme, and anything that needs a decision flagged with a date by which it is needed.

What if we want to change something mid-project?

You can, and it is priced as a variation in writing before the work is done. Changes after a stage is approved cost more than changes before it, which is why the design stage is where we push for decisions.

Do you handle approvals as part of this?

Yes, including the building plan sanction where one is needed. Statutory fees are paid by the owner at cost.
