Vastu planning

Orientation, entrance, room placement and proportion, planned into the design from the start and balanced against the plot, the rules and the climate.

An input to the plan, not a correction to it

A large share of the families we design for want the house planned to Vastu. That is a legitimate brief, and the studio treats it as one: a set of requirements about orientation, entrance, room placement and proportion that sits alongside every other requirement — the plot, the bye-laws, the budget, the sun, and how the household actually lives.

What decides whether it works is when it enters. Brought in at the first sketch, a Vastu brief shapes the plan and costs nothing. Brought in after the layout is frozen, it becomes a list of corrections, and corrections to a frozen plan are expensive, awkward, or both — a kitchen moved after the plumbing is drawn, an entrance shifted after the setback is fixed, a staircase turned after the structure is designed.

What we work with

The principles clients most often bring us concern the direction the entrance faces, which corner the kitchen occupies, where the master bedroom and the pooja space sit, where water and heavy mass belong, and the proportion and regularity of the plot itself. We plan to those requirements where the site allows it.

Where the site does not allow it, we say so rather than pretending. A plot whose only road access is on the wrong side, a flat whose walls cannot move, a narrow frontage that forces the entrance — these are real constraints, and no drawing dissolves them. What a design can do is get as close as the plot honestly permits, and tell you plainly where it fell short and why. That is more use to you than a reassurance.

Where Vastu and good building agree

Several Vastu preferences line up neatly with how a building ought to be planned in this climate, and where they do, you get both for the price of one: keeping heavy, closed mass to the south and west shades the faces that take the worst afternoon sun; opening the north and east brings in light that does not carry much heat; cross ventilation through aligned openings is both a Vastu concern and the cheapest cooling a house in Delhi NCR will ever get.

Where a Vastu requirement and a practical one genuinely conflict — a bedroom that would bake, a kitchen with no ventilation, a layout that wastes a third of the plot — we put the conflict in front of you with both options drawn, and you choose. We do not quietly resolve it in either direction.

Working with your own Vastu consultant

Many clients already have a pandit or consultant they trust, and that is the easiest version of this work. Send us their requirements, in writing or in a sketch, and we design to them. Where something is not buildable or not permitted by the bye-laws, we come back to you and to them with what is, rather than drawing something that cannot be sanctioned.

We are architects. We plan to the brief; we do not issue Vastu rulings, and we will not tell you that a particular arrangement will or will not change your fortunes. What we will tell you is exactly what the plot, the rules and the budget allow.

FAQ

Questions we are asked

Can you design a Vastu-compliant house on any plot?

No, and anyone who says otherwise is selling something. Road position, plot shape, the direction services enter from and the bye-laws all constrain what is possible. On most plots a great deal of a Vastu brief can be met; on some, parts of it cannot. We tell you which at the plot study, before you have paid for a design.

We already have a Vastu consultant. Will you work with them?

Yes, and it is the arrangement we prefer. You give us their requirements and we plan to them, raising anything that is not buildable or not permitted so that the three of us resolve it on paper rather than on site.

Our house is already built. Can it be corrected?

Sometimes, within interiors — layout, entry sequence, where functions sit, storage, lighting. What cannot change without serious cost is structure, drainage and the position of the entrance in a load-bearing wall. We will look at the plan and tell you which of your concerns are addressable and which are not, rather than proposing work that spends money for the sake of it.

Does Vastu planning cost extra?

It is part of the planning, not a separate line, when it is in the brief from the start. What does cost extra is reworking a design that was finished without it.

Do you follow Vastu even when a client does not ask?

No. We design to the brief we are given. Several things Vastu prefers — shaded south and west faces, open north and east, cross ventilation — are simply good building in this climate, so they show up in our plans regardless, but they are there because they work, not as a silent assumption about what you want.
