How to use it

Choose a room and press Suggest a layout. Each press shows another arrangement for your room, and every one keeps clear floor for use: two feet beside and beyond a bed, two and a half in front of a wardrobe or desk, the TV square to the sofa and six to eleven feet from it with only the coffee table between, and room round a dining table for its chairs. A kids’ room puts a single, bunk, loft or trundle bed lengthwise in a corner, with a wardrobe, a desk and four feet square of open floor to play on. Bunk and loft beds also need room to sit up in the top bed: three feet above the mattress, so a ceiling of at least 7.6 feet for a bunk and 8.6 feet for a loft bed. Enter your ceiling height and a bed that leaves less turns red. A child on the top bed can also reach a ceiling fan, so with the ceiling fan ticked, a bunk or loft bed must stay a foot clear of the circle the blades sweep, drawn dashed on the plan for the usual 1200 mm fan. It starts in the middle of the room; drag it, or select it and use the arrow keys, to where the fan point is in yours. A loft bed has a desk or a wardrobe built in underneath, with two and a half feet clear in front for the chair or the doors, so the room needs no second one. A trundle bed keeps three feet clear beside it for the trundle to pull out into; select it and press Pull the trundle out to see the second bed, and anything in its way turns red. Twin beds go side by side with a bedside table between them, or in corners of facing walls across a shared two-and-a-half-foot aisle, and get the desk and play space only where they still fit. A kitchen is offered L-shaped, parallel, U-shaped or straight, with counters two feet deep and three and a half feet clear in front of them, a foot and a half of counter either side of the hob, and the fridge, sink and hob four to nine feet apart, the usual work-triangle guideline. If no arrangement keeps that space, it says so rather than cram one in. Windows, switches and columns are not drawn, so check a layout against your own room.

Drag a piece to move it, or select it and use the arrow keys, holding Shift for bigger steps. Press R or the Turn button to turn it a quarter, and Delete to take it out. Select a piece to see its gap to the nearest wall or piece on every side.

The door sits in the bottom wall and swings into the room. Anything in its arc turns red, because a wardrobe that fits on paper but stops the door opening does not fit.

About the sizes

Every piece uses a common size from Indian furniture shops, so a queen bed is 5 by 6½ feet and a three-seat sofa 7 by 3. Your own furniture may differ by a few inches, which is often exactly the margin that decides whether something fits, so measure what you have before you buy.

Remember the space furniture needs in use as well as at rest: chairs pulled out from a dining table, a wardrobe’s doors swung open, room to walk round the foot of a bed. The gaps shown are clear floor, before any of that.

Planning a whole home?

A good layout is decided at the drawing stage, with doors, windows and switches placed around the furniture rather than the other way round.
