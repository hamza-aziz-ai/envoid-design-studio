What to try

Keep the window facing south and switch between the shortest and the longest day at noon. In December the sun is low, under 40° up, and pours deep into a south-facing room just when the warmth is welcome. In June it stands almost overhead, nearly 85° up, and barely gets past the sill. That single fact is why a south-facing living room in Delhi NCR is so prized, and why a modest overhang above a south window shades it in summer without darkening it in winter.

Now turn the window to face west and play a June day. The afternoon sun comes in low and long, straight into the room at the hottest hour. West glass is the hardest to shade, because the sun is too low for an overhang to stop it.

How it works

The sun's position is calculated in your browser for the studio's own location in Sector 5, Vaishali, using the standard NOAA solar equations. They are accurate to about a minute of time, which is far finer than any window decision needs. The room is 12 by 12 feet with a 4-foot-wide window from 3 to 7 feet above the floor, and the bright patch on the floor is where direct sun lands through it.

The model deliberately ignores everything around the window: neighbouring buildings, trees, balconies, a chajja above it, the depth of the wall, and the glass itself. On a real plot those decide far more than orientation alone, and accounting for them is what a proper sun-path and daylight study does.
